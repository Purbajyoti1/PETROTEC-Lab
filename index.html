About

Dr. Purbajyoti Phukon

Assistant Professor · Fulbright Fellow · Department of Applied Geology, Dibrugarh University

Dr. Purbajyoti Phukon is a geologist specializing in metamorphic petrology, petrochronology, and tectonic processes operating in convergent continental settings. His research focuses on understanding the evolution of the continental crust through investigations of metamorphic and magmatic processes, crustal melting, deformation, and exhumation.

His research experience encompasses the Himalayan orogenic belt and high-grade Precambrian crystalline terrains. He employs an integrated approach that combines field geology, petrography, mineral chemistry, microstructural analysis, trace-element geochemistry of minerals, phase-equilibrium modeling, and monazite, zircon, and titanite U–Pb geochronology to investigate the temporal and petrological evolution of crustal processes. His broader research interests also include metamorphic CO₂ fluxes and critical metal enrichment.

Group

Research Group: PETROTEC Lab

The overarching objective of the laboratory is to unravel the processes governing crustal melting, mineral growth, deformation, exhumation, and fluid–rock interaction, and to explore their implications for geological carbon cycling and critical metal enrichment. Through integrated field-based, petrological, geochemical, thermodynamic, and geochronological investigations, the laboratory seeks to address fundamental questions in Earth Sciences, with particular emphasis on the Himalayan orogen and Precambrian crystalline terrains of Northeast India.
